An ergonomic workspace on a small budget: where to start

Ergonomics does not start with the most expensive chair. Most improvements come from rearranging what you own and a few small purchases. This order helps each unit of money do the most good.

1. Free: adjust what you own

Set your chair so your feet rest on the floor. Put the screen in front of you, about an arm’s length away. Bring the keyboard close and fold its rear feet. None of this costs anything.

2. Creative and nearly free

  • A box or a few sturdy books under your feet instead of a footrest.
  • A small cushion or rolled towel behind your lower back if the chair has no lumbar support.
  • Books under the monitor or laptop to raise the screen.
  • A phone reminder to get up every half hour.

3. A separate keyboard and mouse

If you work on a laptop, this is the cheapest and most effective purchase. With a separate keyboard you can raise the screen without raising your hands too. Cornell University recommends exactly this for sustained laptop use.

4. Raise the screen

A laptop stand or monitor arm brings the screen to eye level. On a tight budget, books do the job for now.

5. The chair, then the desk

A good chair is a big investment, best made once you know exactly which adjustments you need. A standing desk is usually the most expensive item and the last step. Before either, read the buying guide for that category.

One simple rule

Buy the thing that solves a specific problem you feel today: a tired neck, wrists or back. Buying several items for one problem, or something only because it looks good, spends your budget without effect.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.
