How to use a standing desk: how long to stand and sit

After buying a standing desk, many people either stand all day and get tired, or stop raising it after a few weeks. Both miss the real point: changing your body’s position regularly.

Standing does not replace sitting

Standing for a long time holds your body in one position, just like sitting for a long time. Cornell’s guidance says plainly that simply standing is insufficient: what matters is changing posture and moving.

A simple pattern

Cornell suggests this: in each half hour, sit for about 20 minutes in a good posture, stand for about 8 minutes and move for about 2 minutes. They also stress that the exact numbers are not critical; what matters is changing position roughly every 20 to 30 minutes and moving for a couple of minutes.

Start gently

If you are not used to standing, begin with short periods and build up. Comfortable shoes and an anti-fatigue mat make standing easier. If you have foot or back pain, talk to a professional before changing your routine.

The right height in both positions

When you raise the desk, the elbow rule still applies: forearms roughly parallel to the floor and shoulders relaxed. Check the monitor height too; if it is on an arm, you may need to adjust it as well.

Make the habit easy

  • Save your sitting and standing heights in the desk’s memory presets.
  • Take short tasks such as phone calls or email standing up.
  • Set a simple reminder on your phone or computer.
  • Use the two minutes of movement to actually walk: refill your water or do a few stretches.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.
