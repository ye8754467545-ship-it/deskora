Monitor arm buying guide: VESA, weight and mounting

A monitor arm frees your desk and lets you put the screen at exactly the right height and distance: the top at or slightly below eye level, as OSHA recommends. But an arm that does not match your monitor or desk either will not mount or will not hold the screen.

1. The VESA pattern on the monitor

Most monitors have four screw holes on the back at a standard spacing called the VESA pattern. The most common patterns for desktop monitors are 75 × 75 and 100 × 100 mm. Check your monitor’s specifications. If it has no VESA holes at all, look for an adapter made for that model.

2. Monitor weight, without its stand

Most arms use a gas spring designed for a specific weight range. Find the monitor’s weight without its stand on the specification sheet. If it is heavier than the maximum it will sink; if it is lighter than the minimum it will drift up. Curved and ultrawide monitors are often heavier and need a suitable arm. Check the supported screen size too.

3. The desk edge: clamp or grommet

Arms usually fix with a clamp on the desk edge or with a bolt through a cable grommet hole. Measure your desktop thickness and compare it with the clamp’s range. There must be room behind the desk for the clamp, and the desktop must be strong enough to take the load; be careful with glass or very thin tops.

Other points

  • Reach: can the arm bring the screen 50 to 100 cm from you?
  • Height: can the top of the screen reach your eye level, especially with a standing desk?
  • Rotation to portrait, if you read long documents or code.
  • Cable routing inside the arm, for a tidier desk.
  • For two monitors, a dual arm or two single arms; two singles are more flexible.

Do you need an arm at all?

If your current monitor stand adjusts in height and brings the screen to eye level, an arm may not be essential. Our article “Monitor arm or the original stand?” answers that question.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: OSHA eTool: Monitors
