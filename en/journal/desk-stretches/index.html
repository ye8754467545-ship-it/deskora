A few simple stretches at your desk

Short movement breaks are an important part of healthy desk work. Cornell University’s guidance suggests changing position roughly every 20 to 30 minutes and moving for a couple of minutes. You can do these three simple moves in those breaks.

Before you start: these moves should be gentle and painless. Never push into pain. If you have an injury, recent surgery or ongoing pain, check with a doctor or physiotherapist first.

1. Gentle neck tilt

Sit or stand tall with relaxed shoulders. Slowly tilt your head to one side, bringing your ear towards your shoulder without lifting the shoulder. Stay for a few slow breaths, then do the other side.

2. Overhead reach

Stand up, interlace your fingers and slowly raise your arms overhead with your palms facing the ceiling. Lengthen your body a little, hold for a few breaths and lower slowly. It feels good after a long time leaning forward.

3. Shoulder-blade squeeze

Bend your elbows and keep them by your sides. Gently draw your shoulder blades together and slightly down, opening your chest. Hold for a few seconds and release. Repeat a few times.

Most important of all: walk

No stretch replaces walking. Get up, refill your water, go to the window and look into the distance; your eyes get a rest too.

  • Keep every move gentle and painless.
  • Do not hold your breath.
  • Do not bounce or force a stretch.
  • Stop if anything hurts.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: Cornell University Ergonomics Web: Sitting and standing
