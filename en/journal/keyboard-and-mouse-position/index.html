Where to put your keyboard and mouse to spare your wrists

For most of your computer hours, your hands are on the keyboard and mouse. Moving these two by a few centimetres can be the difference between tired wrists and comfortable ones.

Close and centred

Place the keyboard directly in front of you, with the middle of the letter keys (around H and J) in line with your navel. Keep it close enough that your elbows stay at your sides and you do not have to reach.

Height: level with your elbows

According to OSHA, hands, wrists and forearms should be straight, in line and roughly parallel to the floor. If the keyboard is above elbow height your shoulders rise; if it is far below, your wrists bend upward.

Leave the keyboard feet folded

Many keyboards have two small feet at the back that tilt the keyboard towards you. That tilt usually bends your wrists further upward. Cornell University’s guidance goes the other way and suggests a slight negative tilt, with the keyboard sloping gently away from you, for example on a negative-tilt keyboard tray. Without such a tray, at least keep the keyboard flat.

Mouse: beside the keyboard, at the same level

Place the mouse right next to the keyboard and at the same height. If you have to reach for it, a keyboard without a number pad brings the mouse closer. Move it from your forearm rather than only from your wrist.

Palm rests

A soft rest in front of the keyboard is for resting between bursts of typing. While typing, it is better for your hands to move freely without your wrists pressing on the edge. Never rest your wrists on a hard desk edge.

A quick check

  • The keyboard is centred and close.
  • Elbows are at your sides and forearms roughly level.
  • The keyboard is flat or tilted slightly away, not towards you.
  • The mouse is beside the keyboard at the same height.
  • Your wrists are not on a hard desk edge.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: OSHA eTool: Working positionsCornell University Ergonomics Web: Workstation guidelines
