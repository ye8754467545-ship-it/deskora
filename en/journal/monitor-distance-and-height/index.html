How far away and how high should your monitor be?

Where your monitor sits directly affects your neck, shoulders and eyes. A screen that is too low bends your head forward, one that is too high tips your neck back, and one that is too close makes your eyes work harder. Fortunately, a few simple rules solve most cases.

Distance: about an arm’s length

OSHA’s guidance is to place the monitor at least 50 cm (20 inches) from your eyes, with a preferred distance of 50 to 100 cm. The American Academy of Ophthalmology suggests about 25 inches, roughly an arm’s length. The real test is whether you can read all the text with your head and torso upright and your back supported.

If the text is hard to read at that distance, make it larger in your system settings rather than pulling the screen closer. A screen that is too close makes your eyes work harder to converge.

Height: top of the screen at or below eye level

The top line of text should be at or slightly below eye level, so your gaze falls slightly downward. This is what both OSHA and the American Academy of Ophthalmology recommend.

Straight in front of you

Put your main monitor directly in front of you, in line with the keyboard. A screen off to one side keeps your neck twisted for hours. For two screens, see our dual-monitor guide.

Light and reflections

OSHA suggests placing the monitor at right angles to windows, so daylight neither falls on the screen nor into your eyes. Match the screen brightness to the light around you.

When the height will not work

  • If the monitor stand is not tall enough, a monitor arm or even a few sturdy books helps.
  • If you wear bifocal or progressive lenses, you may need the screen lower so you do not tip your head back.
  • For a laptop, a laptop stand with a separate keyboard and mouse is the main fix.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: OSHA eTool: MonitorsAmerican Academy of Ophthalmology: Computers, Digital Devices, and Eye Strain
