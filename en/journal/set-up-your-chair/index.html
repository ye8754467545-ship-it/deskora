Set up your ergonomic chair in six steps

An ergonomic chair arrives with factory settings, not your body’s settings. Many people never try the levers. Follow these six steps in order, because each one affects the next.

1. Seat height

Stand in front of the chair and set the seat at about the bottom of your kneecap. Then sit: your feet should rest fully on the floor, thighs roughly level and knees about level with your hips. If your desk is fixed and high, set the chair to suit the desk and add a footrest.

2. Seat depth

Sit all the way back. A small gap should remain between the front edge of the seat and the back of your knees, usually a few fingers’ width. If the seat edge presses behind your knees, slide the seat back.

3. Lumbar support

The lumbar support should sit in the natural curve of your lower back, neither higher nor lower. If its height or depth adjusts, find the position that feels supportive without pushing.

4. Back angle and tension

Set the back tension so you can lean back slightly and the backrest holds you without effort. If it has a recline lock, you do not have to lock it fully upright; many people are more comfortable leaning back a little.

5. Armrests

Lower the armrests until your shoulders do not lift, and raise them enough for your forearms to rest on them. Aim for an elbow angle between 90 and 120 degrees. If the arms stop you getting close to the desk, lower them or move them aside.

6. Headrest, if there is one

A headrest is for when you lean back, not for pushing your head forward while typing. Set it behind the lower part of your head.

After the chair

Now match the desk, keyboard and monitor to the chair. The desk height and monitor distance guides are the next steps. New settings can feel strange for the first few days; if you are still uncomfortable after a week, adjust again.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: OSHA eTool: Working positions
