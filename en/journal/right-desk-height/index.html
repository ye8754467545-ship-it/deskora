What is the right desk height?

Standard desks have a fixed height designed for an “average person”, but nobody is exactly average. The good news is that you do not need to memorise a number: your body is the measuring stick.

The elbow rule

First set your chair so your feet rest on the floor and your thighs are roughly level. Then relax your shoulders and bend your elbows. The surface your hands work on, meaning the keyboard and mouse, should be close to that height or slightly below, so your forearms stay roughly parallel to the floor.

If your desk is fixed and too high

This is the most common problem. Raise the chair until your elbows are level with the desk, then add a footrest so your feet are not left dangling. Another option is an under-desk keyboard tray. Cornell University’s guidance favours a tray that tilts the keyboard slightly away from you (negative tilt).

If your desk is too low

A low desk pulls your shoulders forward and bends your back. Sturdy desk risers or a different desk are the fix. Do not lower the chair so far that your knees rise above your hips.

For a standing desk

The same rule applies when standing: forearms roughly parallel to the floor and shoulders relaxed. So a standing desk needs a height range that covers both your seated and your standing elbow height. The site’s Body Fit Calculator gives a rough starting point from your height.

In short

  • Measure desk height against your own elbows, not a standard figure.
  • Desk too high: raise the chair and add a footrest.
  • Desk too low: sturdy risers or a different desk.
  • Standing desk: compare its height range with your seated and standing elbow heights.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.
