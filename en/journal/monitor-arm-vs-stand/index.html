Monitor arm vs. monitor stand

Both raise the screen, but they differ in adjustability, installation and cost.

When is a stand enough?

  • Only one person uses the monitor and its position does not change.
  • The monitor’s own stand gives the right height.
  • Your desk edge does not suit a clamp.
  • Your budget is limited.

When is an arm worth it?

  • You move between sitting and standing through the day.
  • Your desk is small and you want clear space under the screen.
  • You have two monitors and want to align them precisely.
  • You sometimes turn the screen toward a colleague or guest.

Three measurements before buying an arm

The monitor’s weight without its stand must be within the arm’s capacity. The monitor needs a VESA pattern. And the desk needs a suitable thickness and edge for a clamp or grommet; thin or glass tops can cause problems.

Permitted screen size alone is not enough. Two monitors of the same size can differ greatly in weight, especially ultrawide and curved models.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.
