Ergonomics for home offices

At home we often work on things not made for work: a dining table, a sofa or the edge of a bed. Before any purchase, apply the simple principles.

Adjust from the bottom up

  • Feet: flat on the floor or a footrest.
  • Thighs: roughly level.
  • Lower back: supported by the backrest.
  • Shoulders: relaxed, not lifted.
  • Elbows: close to your body.
  • Wrists: nearly straight.
  • Screen: its top at or slightly below eye level.

Table too high?

Raise the seat until your elbows are level with the keyboard, and use a footrest if your feet leave the floor. This is a simple way to work at a dining table.

Change your position

Even the best position gets tiring if it stays unchanged for hours. Build in short breaks to get up, walk and look into the distance.

Do not forget light

Place the screen so that a window is not directly behind or in front of it. A window to the side usually gives less glare, and evening lighting should light the room as well as the screen.

When to buy a standing desk

If you work several hours a day and have a fixed space, an adjustable desk is worth considering. Before buying, check its lowest and highest heights with the Body Fit Calculator.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: OSHA, Computer Workstations eTool
