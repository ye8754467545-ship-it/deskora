Setting up a workspace in a small room

Not everyone has a separate study. Many of us work in a bedroom corner or beside the living room. A small space has limits, but with a few good choices you can still follow the same ergonomic rules.

Desk depth versus monitor distance

Shallow desks take less room, but OSHA recommends at least 50 cm between you and the monitor. If the desk is shallow, a monitor arm lets you push the screen back to the rear edge of the desk or slightly beyond. Another option is a laptop on a stand, which takes up less surface.

Free up the desk surface

In a small space, every centimetre of desk counts. A monitor arm, cables gathered under the desk and a wall shelf above it keep the work surface clear.

Do not forget the space behind the chair

The chair needs to roll back a little so you can sit and stand comfortably. If there is a wall or a bed behind it, measure that space before buying a desk.

Light in a small space

If you can, place the desk so the window is to your side. In a dark corner, a desk lamp or monitor light bar is needed so the screen is not the only light.

When the desk is shared

  • If several people share a desk, a chair with quick height adjustment matters more.
  • A standing desk with memory presets stores each person’s height.
  • A folding laptop stand and a compact keyboard can be packed away after work.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.
