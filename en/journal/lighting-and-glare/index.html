Workspace lighting: windows, lamps and screen glare

Good lighting does not simply mean more light. A screen showing the window’s reflection and a room lit only by the screen both tire your eyes.

Window to the side, not behind or in front

OSHA recommends placing the monitor at right angles to windows. With a window behind you, it reflects on the screen; with a window behind the screen, bright light shines straight into your eyes, which keep adjusting between the screen and the bright window. If you cannot move the desk, curtains or blinds help.

Match screen brightness to the room

According to the American Academy of Ophthalmology, a screen brighter than its surroundings makes your eyes work harder. Turn the brightness up by day and down at night. Many systems can do this automatically.

Light on the desk, not on the screen

When you work into the evening, you need a soft light on the desk. Place a lamp so it lights paper and keyboard but does not show in the screen. Monitor light bars are made for exactly this: they light the desk surface and are designed not to reflect on the screen. Before buying, check the specifications and that it fits your monitor’s thickness and curve.

Ceiling lights

A ceiling light directly behind you may show in the screen. If it does, tilt the screen slightly or use indirect light.

When glare persists

  • Change the screen’s vertical tilt slightly.
  • Try a matte filter on a glossy screen.
  • Move the light source, not your head.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: OSHA eTool: MonitorsAmerican Academy of Ophthalmology: Computers, Digital Devices, and Eye Strain
