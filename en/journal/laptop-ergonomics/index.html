Laptop Stands · 5 min read · November 1, 2026

Working on a laptop for hours without neck pain

A laptop is built for portability, not for eight hours at a desk. Cornell University’s guidance sums up the problem: with a laptop, the screen and keyboard are not separate. If the screen is at the right height, the keyboard is too high; if the keyboard is right, the screen is too low.

What a laptop on the desk does

With the laptop flat on the desk, the screen sits well below eye level and your head bends to see it. Your neck then holds the weight of your head in that position for hours.

The fix: screen up, hands down

For sustained use, Cornell recommends a separate keyboard. Put the laptop on a stand so the top of the screen comes close to eye level, and work with a separate keyboard and mouse on the desk. The laptop effectively becomes a monitor.

Fixed or folding stand?

Fixed stands, such as a single piece of bent aluminium, are solid and simple on a desk. Folding stands are light and fit in a bag, which matters if you move between home and office. Check whether the height is adjustable too; a fixed-height stand may not be tall enough for you.

If you do not have a stand yet

A few sturdy books or a box, together with any separate keyboard and mouse you own, makes a difference today. Just make sure the laptop is stable and its vents are not blocked.

Travel and short sessions

  • For short tasks it is fine to use the laptop directly; just take your breaks.
  • Avoid working with the laptop on your lap: your head bends and your hands have nowhere good to go.
  • A folding stand and a compact keyboard in your bag make a temporary office bearable.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.
