Screen eye strain: causes and simple fixes

If your eyes feel dry, tired or blurry after a few hours at a computer, you are not alone. The American Academy of Ophthalmology explains that looking at screens for long periods does not permanently damage your eyes, but it can cause temporary strain, dryness and blur. The good news is that most causes have simple fixes.

Blink

According to the American Academy of Ophthalmology, we normally blink about 15 times a minute, but only about 5 to 7 times a minute while using screens. Blinking keeps the surface of the eye moist. Make a conscious effort to blink more; even a small note beside the screen helps.

Look into the distance

Every so often, look up from the screen at something far away. A common reminder is “20-20-20”: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 m) away for about 20 seconds. A window is ideal.

Brightness and contrast

If your screen is much brighter than its surroundings, your eyes work harder. Match the screen brightness to the room and raise the contrast if needed. A bright screen in a dark room is tiring; a soft light on the desk helps.

Reduce reflections

Daylight or lamp light reflecting on the screen tires your eyes. Place the screen at right angles to windows, and if glare persists, a matte screen filter is another option.

Distance and angle

Keep the screen about an arm’s length away, with your gaze slightly downward. Our monitor distance and height guide covers this in more detail.

When to see a doctor

If your eyes are constantly red, blurry or watery, become sensitive to light or hurt, see an eye doctor. If you wear glasses, ask whether lenses for computer distance would help you.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.

Sources: American Academy of Ophthalmology: Computers, Digital Devices, and Eye StrainOSHA eTool: Monitors
