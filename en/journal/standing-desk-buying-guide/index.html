Standing desk buying guide: seven things to check

A standing desk is one of the most expensive pieces of a workspace, and store photos make them all look alike. The real differences are in the numbers on the specification sheet. Check these seven things before you buy.

1. Height range, against your body

This is the most important number. The desk’s lowest height must suit you seated and its highest must suit you standing. Compare both with your own elbow height (see our desk height guide). For very tall or short people, this alone can rule a desk out. Remember that the desktop’s thickness adds to the frame height.

2. Two-stage or three-stage legs

Three-stage legs have three nested columns and usually offer a wider height range than two-stage legs. If you are around average height, two-stage legs may be entirely sufficient.

3. One motor or two

On dual-motor desks each leg has its own motor. This generally gives a higher lifting capacity and smoother movement. Single-motor desks are simpler, but compare their capacity and speed carefully on the specification sheet.

4. Lifting capacity

Add up everything on the desk: monitors, monitor arm, computer, speakers and the desktop itself. That total should sit comfortably below the stated capacity.

5. Stability at full height

Every desk wobbles a little more at its highest setting, but how much varies between models. Foot depth, the frame and crossbars all play a part. If you can, read independent reviews about wobble at standing height; photos do not show it.

6. Desktop size

Depth matters more than it seems. To keep a monitor 50 cm away you need enough depth or a monitor arm. Check the width against your room and the number of screens.

7. Everyday details

  • Memory presets, to switch between two heights with one button.
  • Collision detection, which stops the desk if it hits something on the way down.
  • Noise and speed, where the specification sheet states them.
  • Warranty terms for motors, frame and desktop, which often differ.
  • Delivery and return terms; the desk is heavy.

Finally, a standing desk is not for standing all day. Read our guide to using a standing desk as well.

This is general guidance and does not replace advice from a doctor or specialist. If you have persistent pain, speak to a professional.
